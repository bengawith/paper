# The correct end-to-end route

The immediate task is **not** to regenerate 1,568 aerofoils locally with XFOIL and it is **not** to start optimisation yet.

The correct sequence is:

1. Freeze and reproduce the rejected-paper state.
2. Recover the original raw AirfoilTools downloads or caches from your home PC.
3. Where recovery is impossible, re-ingest the missing AirfoilTools polars conservatively and with full provenance.
4. Rebuild the aerodynamic data in **long form**, retaining genuine (C_L), (C_D), (C_{Dp}), (C_M), upper transition and lower transition values.
5. Rebuild the geometry/CST mapping and leakage-resistant train/calibration/test splits.
6. Train an angle-conditioned surrogate:
   [
   (\text{12 CST parameters},\alpha)
   \rightarrow
   (C_L,\log C_D,C_M)
   ]
7. Tune and calibrate it without touching the locked test set.
8. Build the surface-uncertainty generator.
9. Run deterministic, naïve-robust and trust-constrained risk-aware optimisation.
10. Verify selected results with direct XFOIL and selected higher-fidelity cases.

The submitted paper says the original dataset contained 1,568 UIUC geometries, encoded by 12 CST parameters and mapped to 48 (C_L) values with 48 validity weights. Its aerodynamic labels came from AirfoilTools XFOIL predictions at (Re=10^6).

The original generator has now been located in `curve_gen/scripts/full_set_gen.py`. It confirms that the process downloaded the UIUC coordinate ZIP, requested an AirfoilTools polar for each aerofoil, read the AirfoilTools CSV, and then retained only angle and (C_L). It sampled 48 rows by their row positions across each aerofoil’s source-specific polar rather than preserving the full polar or all its outputs.

AirfoilTools’ actual source polars are considerably richer. They contain:

[
\alpha,\quad C_L,\quad C_D,\quad C_{Dp},\quad C_M,\quad
X_{\mathrm{tr,upper}},\quad X_{\mathrm{tr,lower}}
]

AirfoilTools says its generated polars attempted (-20^\circ) to (+20^\circ) in (0.25^\circ) increments, omitted points that did not converge, and truncated the data after the useful region around maximum and minimum lift. Its examples record Mach zero, (N_{\mathrm{crit}}=9), the Reynolds number and the XFOIL version in the source file. ([Airfoil Tools][1])

That means the old pipeline **discarded (C_D), (C_{Dp}), (C_M) and transition data that were already available**. It also means a missing angle is a solver outcome, not a value that should be silently padded into the new labels.

I have prepared two files for you:

* [Download the complete execution guide](sandbox:/mnt/data/robust_airfoil_v2_execution_guide.md)
* [Download the tested starter repository scaffold](sandbox:/mnt/data/robust_airfoil_v2_starter.zip)

The starter contains a Windows bootstrap, a local-data discovery utility, a legacy-dataset audit, a provenance-preserving AirfoilTools parser, a deliberately limited pilot downloader, schemas, configuration and tests. The supplied parser tests pass.

## 12. Retain more data than the original “clean full curves” filter

Your historical decision to retain only clean, complete curves made sense for a fixed-output network, but it is not ideal for the new model.

It can create selection bias because geometries that are harder for XFOIL to converge may be removed disproportionately. The point-conditioned model can use genuine valid points from a partially covered polar without inventing the missing values.

Create three layers:

1. **Raw layer:** every successfully retrieved source response.
2. **Point-model layer:** every valid real point satisfying the target and metadata checks.
3. **Strict curve subset:** aerofoils with sufficient contiguous coverage for curve-level evaluation and optimisation-domain calibration.

A partial polar may contribute valid pointwise training data but should not automatically qualify for:

* stall extraction;
* full-curve validation;
* optimisation support;
* lower-tail design verification.

# Part VIII — Data-quality rules

## 18. Separate hard exclusions from diagnostic flags

### Hard exclusion from the primary model

Exclude a point when:

* geometry mapping is unresolved;
* source metadata does not match the declared condition;
* (\alpha), (C_L), (C_D), or (C_M) is non-finite;
* (C_D \leq 0);
* the nominal geometry is invalid;
* the CST fit is demonstrably unacceptable under the eventual audited threshold;
* the source row is an unresolved duplicate;
* the raw response cannot be traced and hashed.

### Retain but flag

Do not automatically delete a point merely because it has:

* a large adjacent (C_L) jump;
* a drag spike;
* transition movement;
* a gap before or after it;
* proximity to maximum lift;
* unusual but physically possible (C_M).

These may indicate real separation behaviour or XFOIL instability. Flag them for the strict-subset decision rather than silently smoothing them away.

Suggested flags:

```text
large_delta_cl
large_delta_cd
non_contiguous_alpha
near_cl_max
post_peak
transition_jump
```

# Part IX — Geometry and CST processing

## 20. Normalise each UIUC coordinate file consistently

UIUC explicitly notes that its archive contains both Selig-style and Lednicer-style coordinate ordering, so format detection must be part of the processing rather than assuming every file uses one layout. ([Applied Aerodynamics Group][4])

For every geometry:

1. Read the title and numeric coordinate lines.
2. Detect Selig versus Lednicer ordering.
3. Remove only exact duplicate points initially.
4. Identify leading and trailing edges.
5. Translate and rotate so:
   [
   LE=(0,0),\qquad TE=(1,0)
   ]
6. Scale to unit chord.
7. Ensure ordering:

   ```text
   TE upper → LE → TE lower
   ```
8. Split upper and lower surfaces.
9. Interpolate each surface onto 201 cosine-spaced (x/c) locations.
10. Check:

    * surface crossing;
    * negative local thickness;
    * self-intersection;
    * chord definition;
    * missing leading edge;
    * excessive coordinate discontinuity.
11. Fit the same five-lower/five-upper CST representation with `dz` and `zeta`.
12. Reconstruct the fitted shape.
13. Store:

    * RMS reconstruction error;
    * maximum absolute error;
    * thickness-to-chord;
    * maximum camber;
    * section area;
    * leading-edge radius;
    * trailing-edge thickness.

Do not choose the CST rejection threshold before plotting the full error distribution and inspecting the worst-fitting examples.

# Part X — Leakage-resistant dataset splitting

## 22. Do not split point rows or ordinary airfoil names randomly

Every operating point from one nominal geometry must remain in one partition. Every future perturbation of that geometry must inherit the same partition.

Use:

```text
development: 70% of geometry clusters
calibration: 15% of geometry clusters
locked test: 15% of geometry clusters
```

The roles are:

* **Development:** grouped cross-validation and model fitting.
* **Calibration:** residual/trust calibration after architecture selection.
* **Locked test:** opened once after all model and calibration decisions are frozen.

This is stronger than using one validation set repeatedly for tuning, early stopping and conformal calibration.

---

## 23. Construct geometry clusters

For every normalised geometry:

1. Resample upper and lower surfaces on the same 201-point cosine grid.
2. Concatenate them into a fixed geometry vector.
3. Hash the rounded vector for exact duplicates.
4. Calculate nearest-neighbour RMS ordinate distances.
5. Plot the nearest-distance distribution.
6. Manually inspect at least the 100 closest non-identical pairs.
7. Choose and document a near-duplicate threshold.
8. Form connected components under that threshold.
9. Assign whole components to one split.

Do not select the threshold solely to produce convenient 70/15/15 counts.

Publish:

```text
data/splits/development.csv
data/splits/calibration.csv
data/splits/locked_test.csv
data/splits/split_manifest.json
```

Add a test asserting that no `geometry_cluster_id` appears in more than one partition.

Use named family holdouts such as NACA, Eppler or Selig as additional out-of-distribution stress tests, not as a substitute for geometry-cluster separation.

# Part XI — The new surrogate model

## 24. Primary formulation

At the fixed source condition:

[
Re=10^6,\qquad M=0,\qquad N_{\mathrm{crit}}=9
]

train:

[
f_\theta(\mathbf p,\alpha)
==========================

\left[
\hat C_L,\widehat{\log C_D},\hat C_M
\right]
]

where:

[
\mathbf p=
[
A_{L,0},\ldots,A_{L,4},
A_{U,0},\ldots,A_{U,4},
\Delta z_{LE},
\zeta_{TE}
]
]

This is a 13-input model:

* 12 geometry features;
* one angle-of-attack feature.

Do not include Reynolds number, Mach or (N_{\mathrm{crit}}) as constant-valued input columns. If you later add multiple conditions, then extend the input with:

[
\log_{10}Re,\quad M,\quad N_{\mathrm{crit}}
]

as a separately versioned experiment.

---

## 25. Target treatment

Use:

```text
CL      → standardise
log(CD) → standardise
CM      → standardise
```

Only calculate:

[
\log C_D
]

where (C_D>0). Do not repair invalid drag values by adding an arbitrary epsilon.

Retain but do not initially make primary outputs:

```text
Cdp
Top_Xtr
Bot_Xtr
```

They can later be tested as auxiliary heads. Keep them in the dataset now so the decision remains possible.

---

## 26. Prevent longer polars dominating training

If aerofoil (i) has (n_i) accepted points, assign:

[
w_{i,k}=\frac{1}{n_i}
]

to each of its points, or implement a sampler that:

1. samples a nominal aerofoil uniformly;
2. samples one of its valid operating points uniformly.

Otherwise, an aerofoil with 150 converged rows contributes five times as much loss as one with 30 rows merely because its solver coverage is longer.

## 30. Use grouped inner cross-validation

The locked test remains closed.

Within the development partition, use three or five grouped folds based on `geometry_cluster_id`.

Suggested Optuna search:

```text

residual blocks:     off/on
```

Use a dimensionless grouped-fold objective:

[

\frac{1}{3}
\left(

\right)
]

where each metric is:

1. calculated per nominal aerofoil;
2. averaged across aerofoils;
3. averaged across grouped validation folds.

This prevents large families and long polars from dominating.

# Part XIII — Model uncertainty and trust calibration

## 33. Train a geometry-resampled ensemble

Train five ensemble members using:

* different model seeds;
* bootstrap or group-resampled nominal aerofoils;
* identical frozen architecture and training procedure.

For a candidate:

[
u_{\mathrm{ens}}(\mathbf p,\alpha)
==================================

\operatorname{Std}_m
[
\hat y_m(\mathbf p,\alpha)
]
]

Call this **ensemble disagreement** or **empirical model uncertainty**, not a formally exact epistemic variance.

---

## 34. Calculate geometry-support distance

Scale the CST input using development-set statistics and calculate:

[
d_{\mathrm{kNN}}(\mathbf p)
===========================

\frac{1}{k}
\sum_{j=1}^{k}
|
\tilde{\mathbf p}
-----------------

\tilde{\mathbf p}_{(j)}
|_2
]

On the untouched calibration geometries, model actual residual magnitude as a function of:

* (d_{\mathrm{kNN}});
* ensemble disagreement;
* (\alpha);
* target;
* source coverage.

Define the trusted domain as the region where the calibrated residual quantile remains below a predeclared engineering tolerance.

Do not merely use “within the 95th percentile of training distance” as proof of trustworthiness.

NeuralFoil is a necessary external benchmark because it already returns (C_L), (C_D), (C_M), transition quantities and an `analysis_confidence` output intended to flag sensitive or out-of-distribution optimisation queries. ([GitHub][8])

Benchmark it on exactly the same:

* test geometries;
* angles;
* Reynolds number;
* source targets;
* error metrics;
* candidate rankings.

Do not train your model on NeuralFoil outputs.

# Part XIV — Build the geometric-uncertainty model

## 35. Use actual surface deviations

For a surface coordinate (\mathbf r(x)), define:

[
\mathbf r^{(s)}(x)
==================

\mathbf r(x)
+
\delta n^{(s)}(x)\hat{\mathbf n}(x)
]

with:

[
\delta n^{(s)}(x)
=================

T(x)
\sum_{j=1}^{q}
\sqrt{\lambda_j}\xi_j^{(s)}\phi_j(x)
]

where:

* (\phi_j(x)) are smooth B-spline or Karhunen–Loève modes;
* (\lambda_j) defines the correlation structure;
* (\xi_j) are bounded random coefficients;
* (T(x)) controls the leading/trailing-edge treatment;
* (\hat{\mathbf n}(x)) is the local normal.

Define amplitude unambiguously as:

[
\epsilon
========

\frac{\max_x |\delta n(x)|}{c}
]

and also report RMS displacement.

Initial stress-test levels:

```text
0.1% chord
0.25% chord
0.5% chord
1.0% chord
```

Unless these levels are based on measured manufacturing evidence, describe them as **prescribed bounded correlated geometric uncertainty**, not a measured manufacturing distribution.

---

## 36. Required uncertainty ablations

Compare:

1. Independent percentage perturbation of CST coefficients.
2. Independent coordinate-point noise.
3. Smooth correlated normal displacement.

Also vary:

* displacement amplitude;
* correlation length;
* upper/lower-surface correlation;
* number of retained modes.

This comparison is central to novelty: it tests whether the uncertainty representation materially changes the selected robust design.

For every realisation:

1. Generate the actual perturbed coordinates.
2. Check self-intersection.
3. Check local thickness.
4. Check leading/trailing-edge integrity.
5. Fit back to the 12-CST representation.
6. record CST refit error.
7. Use the CST fit for surrogate inference.
8. Use the **actual displaced coordinates** for direct XFOIL/RANS validation.

---

## 37. Sampling

During optimisation:

```text
M = 64 common scrambled Sobol samples
```

Use antithetic pairs where appropriate:

[
+\boldsymbol\xi,\quad-\boldsymbol\xi
]

Reuse the same sample matrix for every candidate. This is a common-random-number design that reduces comparison noise.

For final evaluation:

```text
M = 2,000 independent samples
```

Run convergence checks at:

```text
16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1000, 2000
```

Monitor convergence of:

* mean;
* standard deviation;
* 5th/95th percentile;
* CVaR;
* constraint-violation probability.

# Part XV — The optimisation problem

## 38. Do not maximise average (C_L) alone

A useful first **pilot** design brief is:

```text
Re = 1,000,000
Mach = 0
Ncrit = 9
reference = a conventional baseline such as NACA 2412,
            after exact source and geometry verification
target CL values = 0.4, 0.7, 1.0
weights = 0.25, 0.50, 0.25
```

The pilot target values are not yet a publication claim. Ava and the authors should approve the final engineering use case before full runs.

For each geometry realisation (s) and service condition (k), solve:

[
C_L(\mathbf p,\boldsymbol\xi_s,\alpha_{k,s})
============================================

C_{L,\mathrm{req},k}
]

within the trusted angle interval.

If no valid root exists, the realisation violates the lift-achievability constraint.

Define:

[
D_s(\mathbf p)
==============

\sum_k
w_k
C_D(
\mathbf p,\boldsymbol\xi_s,\alpha_{k,s}
)
]

Primary Pareto objectives:

[
\min_{\mathbf p} E[D_s]
]

and:

[
\min_{\mathbf p}
\operatorname{CVaR}_{0.95}(D_s)
]

This asks for good average efficiency and good adverse-tail efficiency.

---

## 39. Constraints

Relative to the chosen reference aerofoil, begin with constraints such as:

[
0.98,t_{\mathrm{ref}}
\leq t/c
\leq 1.02,t_{\mathrm{ref}}
]

[
A(\mathbf p)
\geq 0.98 A_{\mathrm{ref}}
]

[
r_{LE}(\mathbf p)
\geq 0.8 r_{LE,\mathrm{ref}}
]

[
C_M(\mathbf p,\alpha_k)
\geq
C_{M,\mathrm{ref}}(\alpha_k)-0.01
]

along with:

* minimum local thickness;
* trailing-edge-thickness limits;
* no surface crossing;
* no self-intersection;
* CST fit validity;
* trusted-support distance;
* ensemble/calibrated error limits;
* target lift achievable under a declared percentage of uncertainty realisations.

These relative constraints keep the first comparison controlled. They can be replaced by application-derived absolute limits once the design brief is agreed.

---

## 40. Compare five designs

You need:

1. Reference aerofoil.
2. Deterministic optimum.
3. Ava’s simple robust baseline using independent CST perturbations.
4. Smooth correlated-surface robust optimum.
5. Trust-calibrated robust Pareto knee.

This lets the paper distinguish:

* the value of robust optimisation;
* the value of a physical uncertainty representation;
* the value of lower-tail risk;
* the value of surrogate trust constraints.

## 41. Optimisers

First debug a scalar objective using SciPy differential evolution.

Then use NSGA-II through `pymoo` for:

[
\min E[D],\qquad
\min \operatorname{CVaR}_{0.95}(D)
]

`pymoo` provides a standard NSGA-II implementation with non-dominated sorting and crowding-distance survival, together with explicit constraint-handling support. ([pymoo][9])

Starting values:

```text

  population = 32–64
  generations = 20–50
  uncertainty samples = 16–32

full candidate run:
  population = 128
  generations = 200
  uncertainty samples = 64
  independent optimiser seeds = at least 10
```

Do not treat 200 generations as automatically sufficient. Continue or stop based on:

* hypervolume convergence;
* front movement;
* constraint-feasible population;
* repeated-seed front agreement.

# Part XVIII — Exact go/no-go gates

## Dataset gate

Proceed only if:

* every processed row links to an immutable raw response and geometry hash;
* source conditions are verified;
* mapping ambiguity is quarantined;
* missing source angles remain missing;
* geometry and CST errors are characterised;
* split leakage tests pass.

## Model gate

Proceed only if:

* one-batch overfit passes;
* grouped validation is materially better than simple baselines;
* (C_D) and (C_M) errors are low enough for the proposed optimisation;
* error does not explode within the intended operating domain;
* calibration residuals are usable;
* NeuralFoil comparison is understood.

## Optimisation gate

Proceed only if:

* all geometry constraints are tested;
* uncertainty estimates converge;
* candidate performance improvements exceed calibrated surrogate error;
* repeated optimiser seeds produce a stable trade-off;
* the optimiser is not selecting unsupported geometries.

## Publication gate

Do not make the central robust-design claim unless:

* the direct XFOIL ranking agrees;
* the lower-tail improvement confidence interval does not overlap zero;
* the effect exceeds sampling and surrogate uncertainty;
* selected RANS or matched higher-fidelity evidence supports the main ordering;
* the result remains under reasonable tolerance/correlation-length sensitivity analyses.
